# Gap note for `scotland-ai-split-zones`: behind-the-meter (BTM) supply for training sites

*Written 20 September 2026 in the sibling project "OFGEM NESO AI Revision," because this session cannot write directly into `scotland-ai-split-zones` (a separate claude.ai Project). Self-contained — written to be read without the rest of that project's context. Source detail and full verification trail live in `evidence-log.md`, Thread "Energy-cost and curtailment-arbitrage feasibility check" (EC-01 through EC-09), in the same project as this note.*

## Why this is relevant to split-zones specifically

Split-zones' Stage 5/6 work models a 100MW EHV training site and a 5MW HV inference site as **grid-connected** loads, and its headline cross-archetype finding is that TNUoS dominates training-site value while DUoS dominates inference-site value. That finding implicitly assumes all of a site's power is imported over the public network, since TNUoS and DUoS are both charges for using that network. Behind-the-meter (BTM) supply — power taken directly from a co-located or private-wire-connected generator, without crossing the public network — is a structurally different arrangement, not a variant of the grid-connected case, and doesn't appear to have been modelled in the Stage 5/6 work (based on what's visible from this project's summary of it; the full split-zones documents weren't directly checked from this session).

If any part of Scotland's real or hypothetical training siting uses BTM supply, that changes which network charges actually apply to the site, which is directly the variable Stage 5/6's mirror finding is built on.

## What was checked, and what it found

**1. BTM's actual legal/commercial basis.** Private-wire (BTM) supply operates under the Electricity (Class Exemptions from the Requirement for a Licence) Order 2001 — a licence-exemption route, not a new or special AI-specific mechanism. The supply agreement (typically a PPA) has to be carefully drafted to avoid drifting outside the exemption's terms (mainly via resale restrictions on the consumer).

**2. What it actually saves — and this corrects an assumption worth flagging if it's been made elsewhere.** The main financial benefit of going BTM, per an industry (Scottish Renewables) briefing, is avoiding **policy-related costs** — Contracts for Difference (CfD) and Renewables Obligation (RO) levies, and standing charges. The same source states there are **"limited actual savings regarding distribution charges (DUoS) and transmission charges (TNUoS), except for some avoided transmission costs for non-transmission-connected assets."** In other words: BTM is not primarily a way to escape TNUoS for a training site. If split-zones' Stage 5/6 TNUoS-dominance finding gets revisited with a BTM scenario, the expected effect size on TNUoS specifically should be modelled as small, not large, based on this source — the bigger avoided-cost lever is CfD/RO/policy charges, which sit outside the TNUoS/DUoS framework Stage 5/6 already models.

**3. Feasibility constraints — geographic and by generation type.** Private wire requires the generator and consumer to be genuinely close: the same briefing gives a rough practical maximum of 5-10km, driven by cabling and land-consent costs. Only **hydro, solar, and onshore wind** near the end-user are described as typically viable. **Offshore wind is described as "completely unfeasible"** for private wire, because offshore assets connect at transmission level and are financed and structured in a way incompatible with a direct private-wire arrangement.

**4. The finding that matters most for scope.** The three most-curtailed 2025 Scottish wind assets identified in the sibling B4/B6 study — Seagreen, Moray East, and Moray West — are **all offshore wind**. If (3) is right, none of them could supply a training site via BTM/private wire. Any genuine BTM opportunity in northern Scotland would need to be built around a *different*, onshore curtailed generator sited within roughly 5-10km of a candidate training site — a real but much narrower and more specific proposition than "site near curtailed wind in the north," and one that hasn't been checked against any specific site or generator pairing.

**5. Blackdog, checked and resolved (update, 20 September 2026).** The one project in the wider AI pipeline that's actually sited north of B4 (Blackdog, Aberdeenshire) is described in press coverage as exploring a "private-wire to offshore wind" connection — a description that sat in apparent tension with (3) above. This has now been checked directly (logged as EC-10 in `evidence-log.md`), and the tension resolves rather than deepens: press coverage (digit.fyi) describes this as an exploratory discussion, not a confirmed arrangement — "Ashfield and TechRE are exploring the potential for private-wire connections," with "discussions... taking place between Ashfield and Cerulean Winds... over the feasibility of running a link between Blackdog and the initial Aspen windfarm." The same coverage states the initial 600MW will come from "both renewable and grid connected power" — an explicit hybrid, not pure BTM. Cross-referencing the named counterparty against this study's own SSEN Transmission register data finds Cerulean Winds already listed there as "Cerulean Winds INTOG 1 — 600MW 'Demand;Wind Offshore' at Hurlie 400kV, Awaiting Consents" — a conventional grid-connected demand application, the same pattern as other offshore-adjacent projects in that register (Green Volt, CENOS), not an off-network private wire. Taken together, Blackdog reads as consistent with, not a genuine exception to, the general offshore-infeasibility finding: what's being explored is press-framed and aspirational, and the one confirmed regulatory detail for the named counterparty is a standard grid connection. **Still not independently confirmed:** whether Hurlie substation and the Blackdog site are the same or geographically linked project, and whether "Aspen" windfarm is the same asset as "Cerulean Winds INTOG 1" — the names are consistent with each other but not verified as identical from primary documents. This is close enough to settled that Blackdog should no longer be treated as an open counterexample to the offshore-infeasibility finding, though the residual identity/geography question could still be worth closing if a specific BTM scenario near Blackdog is ever modelled.

**6. Training's genuine latency-insensitivity, independently confirmed.** Separately from the cost question: training workloads (unlike inference) don't need to be near end-users and can in principle be sited wherever power is cheapest or most available — an industry source cites GPT-4's training run in Des Moines, Iowa, chosen for power availability. This is a genuine, independently-sourced fact split-zones' existing training/inference archetype distinction can draw on directly, separate from whether any specific BTM cost arithmetic works out.

**7. Whether NESO's own forecasting has priced any of this in.** NESO's own demand-forecasting team has stated, in direct correspondence held privately, that as a rule they forecast little AI training in the UK due to high energy prices. A real UK government scheme (the November 2025 "Delivering AI Growth Zones" policy) offers Scotland-sited data centres a discount of up to £24/MWh (for a 500MW site) on the *system-charge* portion of their bill — separate from and smaller than the BTM/private-wire question above, since it's a discount on grid-imported power, not a BTM mechanism. A check of NESO's public Future Energy Scenarios (Nov 2025) and its Data Centres document found no mention of this scheme, suggesting (not proving) it isn't yet priced into the "little AI training" forecast. A direct clarifying question to NESO is drafted (in the B4/B6 study design doc) but not yet sent.

## What this means for split-zones, concretely

- **Don't assume BTM materially changes the TNUoS-dominance finding for training sites** without modelling it explicitly — the available evidence suggests the TNUoS/DUoS saving specifically is small; the real BTM saving is on CfD/RO/policy costs, a different charge category Stage 5/6's existing framework may not currently break out at all.
- **If a BTM training-site scenario is modelled**, it should be scoped to onshore wind, solar, or hydro generation within ~5-10km of the candidate site — not offshore wind, and not "somewhere in the curtailed north" in general.
- **The three headline curtailed assets used throughout the sibling B4/B6 study (Seagreen, Moray East, Moray West) are the wrong anchor points for a BTM scenario specifically**, since they're offshore. A BTM-focused piece of work would need to identify onshore curtailed (or curtailment-adjacent) generation candidates instead — this hasn't been done, in either project.
- **Blackdog's actual connection arrangement is worth verifying directly** before it's cited as a working example of BTM-to-offshore-wind, given the apparent tension with the general infeasibility finding above.
- **The checkpointing/timescale-mismatch work already done in split-zones (Stage 5/6) is directly reusable** for a BTM scenario's intermittency problem (what happens during low-wind hours) — this doesn't need to be re-derived, just applied to the BTM case specifically.

## What hasn't been checked, and would need split-zones' own tools to do properly

- A quantified BTM economics model for a specific training-site/generator pairing — capex for the private-wire cable and connection, the negotiated offtake price needed to beat the generator's curtailment-payment counterfactual, and the resulting all-in £/MWh compared to both grid-supplied and AI-Growth-Zone-discounted alternatives. This needs the "physics before economics" and Assumptions Ledger discipline split-zones already has built, applied to a genuinely new scenario type.
- Whether any specific onshore curtailed generator in the north-of-B4 area is actually within private-wire range of a plausible training-site location — a real, checkable geographic question, not yet asked.
- Full text verification of the AI Growth Zones policy paper's funding mechanism claim ("no additional cost to other billpayers") — asserted by government, not independently modelled here or in the sibling project.
